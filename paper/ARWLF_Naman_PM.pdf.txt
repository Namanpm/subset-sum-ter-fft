Wide Leaf Filtering for Exact Ternary Subset Sum Root Reporting
NAMAN PM
Representation algorithms for random Subset Sum generate large lists of signed vectors and repeatedly join them under modular
constraints. At the final ternary merge, a short arithmetic projection can admit a large number of candidates even when the full relation
has no solutions. We study exact arithmetic root reporting with inexpensive rejection ahead of candidate recovery. The Adaptive Radix
Wide-Leaf Filter (ARWLF) partitions residual keys into fixed thirteen-bit leaves, routes source pairs with explicit carries, and tests byte
occupancy in the target leaf. An AVX2 implementation evaluates one source entry against sixteen entries at a time; surviving pairs
undergo full quotient comparison and original-residue verification. On an Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX, ARWLF achieves geometric-mean
speedups of 53.1 over the tiled baseline on fourteen calibration microproblems and 52.9 on fifteen microproblems from three holdout
roots at dimension 108. Against a baseline that already rejects full-width mismatches before the shared output sink, the corresponding
gains are 32.8 and 32.4. Controlled source and target thinning identify both the occupancy crossover and the contribution of candidate
suppression. Sparse dimension-72 roots favor a different reporter. A complete prepared dimension-108 root with one constructed
positive target takes 59.6 minutes in one ARWLF pass. The method preserves exact arithmetic reporting and entry multiplicity;
production binary-root certification remains separate. Its contribution is a finite-size improvement in pair-processing cost, with no
change to the asymptotic Subset Sum exponent.

1 Introduction
The complexity of a representation algorithm is determined by more than the sizes of its final lists. Intermediate
arithmetic matches must be discovered, checked for coordinate compatibility, and recovered as original entries. A
merge that is cheap in terms of accepted parents can therefore be expensive on the machine: a short projection may
generate many candidates, and nearly all of them can fail the remaining conditions. This distinction has appeared in
the analysis of representation methods themselves. Becker, Coron, and Joux explicitly account for the intermediate list
before filtering, rather than charging only the final filtered list [ 5]. It also creates a practical optimization question:
when many inexpensive pair tests are unavoidable, how much work should accompany each test?
This paper addresses that question at the final ternary root merge of a classical Ternary Representation Technique
(TER) construction. Li’s depth-three algorithm forms eighteen logical level-three lists, then nine level-two lists, three
level-one lists, and a final root list [10]. The root arithmetic is a modular three-list reporting problem. Its large source
families admit billions of logical pairs per family cell at the evaluated dimension. Testing every pair with an expensive
membership operation, or recovering every match of a short projection before checking the remaining bits, places the
cost in the wrong part of the computation.
The Adaptive Radix Wide-Leaf Filter changes this cost distribution. Low residual bits route a pair to exactly one
target leaf. Independent occupancy maps for the next bytes reject a pair whenever a required byte is absent. The maps
are deliberately weaker than full equality: they summarize target bytes without retaining their correlations. That loss of
information is useful because a small, register-friendly test can reject most pairs before original entry identities or wide
residues are processed. Exact comparison remains on the survivor path.

The central implementation choice is to organize the scan around a target leaf. Its occupancy tables stay fixed
while source rows traverse their compatible partner leaves. Carry handling becomes two contiguous ranges, and the
hot operation adds one source value to sixteen sixteen-bit lanes. Additional bytes and all remaining quotient bits are
checked only after the initial lane mask has been extracted. The design has one terminal radix level; it does not search
for a different radix at runtime.
The evaluation separates several questions that are easy to conflate. First, does the reporter agree with exact
authorities? Second, does it improve prepared root reporting on authentic captured lists? Third, which geometry
produces the improvement? Fourth, how much of the gain survives a stronger full-width baseline? Finally, does a
dispatcher choose a suitable reporter across regimes? The answers are different. Dense dimension-108 microproblems
show large and reproducible relative gains, including approximately 32-fold gains over the strengthened baseline. Sparse
dimension-72 roots favor a queued position-and-tag reporter. The frozen dispatcher works well on the dense authentic
cells but incurs substantial overhead on tiny cells and excludes some controlled cases where ARWLF would win.
The contributions are an exact leaf-routing and byte-filtering formulation for TER root reporting; a faithful vector
realization with explicit carry, overflow, padding, and identity semantics; a cost analysis that includes survivor storage
and recovery; and a matched experimental study with separate calibration and holdout roots, occupancy controls,
and target-density ablations. The result concerns a prepared root reporter. It is neither an end-to-end TER timing
claim nor a new Subset Sum exponent. That scope still permits a substantial algorithm-engineering contribution: the
measured improvement is obtained by changing when detailed candidate work occurs, rather than by weakening the
mathematical decision.

Arithmetic equality is still only one level of acceptance. The common authority recomputes original residue equality
modulo2𝑛 and applies an attached coordinate-closure and digit-count predicate. Original identities are preserved even
when values coincide. Reporting arithmetic triples, satisfying that attached predicate, deduplicating parent vectors, and
verifying a final binary witness are distinct operations. The principal exactness claim in this paper is full quotient and
original-residue reporting, with the residue-exact identity multiset used as a differential gate.
The benchmark’s attached structural predicate is a finite test-parent predicate, not the production binary-root
predicate. Its negative, zero, and positive counts are (2, 56, 14) at dimension 72 and (2, 86, 20) at dimension 108;
production roots require(0,𝑛/ 2,𝑛/ 2). Authentic level-one vectors each have signed digit sum 𝑛/6, so a triple has
signed digit sum 𝑛/2, whereas the attached predicate requires 𝑛/6. Authentic triples therefore cannot satisfy that
predicate. Structural counters in the experiments are reported only with this meaning. This boundary does not change
the arithmetic join being timed, but it rules out interpreting those counters as production-root certification.
3 Baselines and the dense candidate problem
3.1 The tiled projected reporter
The baseline, K2, evaluates source pairs in eight-by-eight tiles. It retains a nineteen-bit residual projection after the
six family bits. For a projected sum 𝑣, its vector kernel tests a coarse bitmap whose bit represents a block of eight
possible residual values. A passing lane is drained to scalar code, which recomputes𝑣, performs exact membership in
the nineteen-bit target bitmap, and expands every original target posting with that projected value. The shared sink
then performs the remaining arithmetic and structural checks.
This is an exact arithmetic reporter when the downstream authorities are attached. Its early test is a necessary
condition, not the final mathematical decision. The baseline is useful precisely because it preserves the original identity
and multiplicity contract while exposing the cost of a short projection. Each target family uses a 64 KiB exact bitmap
and an 8 KiB coarse bitmap. Across sixty-four families those two bitmap classes occupy 4.5 MiB, excluding postings,
indexes, and authority data.
When large families populate the nineteen-bit residual space densely, the projected match rate can be substantial
even though equality across forty-seven or sixty-nine quotient bits is rare. Every projected match invokes target-posting
recovery and detailed downstream work. The bottleneck is not evidence that the vector kernel is poorly implemented;
it is a consequence of the information retained by the early projection and the point at which discarded information is
restored.
3.2 A stronger full-width baseline
K2-WIDE uses the same tile sweep and projected target directory, but compares the full retained residual and any extra
high bits before calling the shared sink. It therefore tests a narrower hypothesis than the original baseline comparison:
are gains explained solely by avoiding sink calls for low-width false candidates? K2-WIDE removes many such calls, yet
still visits the target postings in each surviving projected group and retains the original coarse pair-processing path.
The wide residual addition is safe in the supported domain because 𝑊≤ 58; two residuals and one carry fit in
fifty-nine bits. The carry out of the𝑊 -bit result is added to the separately stored high quotient parts. Both K2-WIDE and
ARWLF use this full-width comparison. Their reported acceptance therefore agrees at the same downstream authority
level even though early candidate counters differ from K2’s.

3.3 The queued sparse reporter
QUEUE-DENSE splits the nineteen-bit projection into a thirteen-bit position and a six-bit tag. For one source query
it intersects translated target-position occupancy with source-position occupancy, compacts nonzero bitmap words,
then resolves tags by a carry-aware cyclic rotation of sixty-four-bit tag masks. Group directories expand the matching
relation to original postings. Full-width residue and structural decisions remain downstream.
The position space is fixed at 8,192 slots. The implementation’s distinct-residual and hit capacity is 65,536 per relevant
family; the dispatcher checks that capacity before admitting the reporter. The dimension-72 roots satisfy it, while the
evaluated dimension-108 roots do not. QUEUE-DENSE is thus an informative legal alternative in the sparse regimes,
rather than a missing timing entry that can be treated as zero runtime.

4.4 Exact recovery and multiplicity
Pairs surviving all byte maps are buffered for one target leaf. Each is compared with every target posting in that leaf.
The resolver first checks the declared mid projection, then all𝑊 residual bits, then the additional𝑄−𝐹 high bits with
the propagated carry. Only a full quotient match reaches the common sink.
The sink independently checks a low-thirty-two-bit quotient relation, recomputes equality of the original residue
sum and root target modulo2𝑛, and applies the attached vector predicate described in Section 2.3. A residue-exact triple
can fail that predicate. The residue-exact multiset used for differential comparison is captured before the structural
decision; it is not a list of verified binary Subset Sum solutions. For constructed records, even consistency between
coordinate data and stored residues is an additional property that these checks do not establish.
Every posting remains distinct. If equal-valued entries have different identities, the resolver enumerates their separate
matches. The reporter performs no canonical-parent deduplication. This is necessary both for equivalence with the
baselines and for charging output-heavy cases correctly.

6.3 Memory
Target summaries require4· 256bits, or 128 bytes, per leaf. Allocating all 8,192 summaries uses 1 MiB per touched
target family and at most 64 MiB for all sixty-four. Source partitions, row addends, lane slots, full keys, and the pending
mask array add storage linear in their prepared entries plus fixed per-family leaf directories.
ARWLF avoids a complete table of all source-pair sums. It nevertheless stores the pairs surviving one target leaf, and
that vector retains its capacity across leaves. With𝑆max =max𝑘𝑆𝑘, its auxiliary storage is𝑂(𝑁+𝑓𝐻+𝑆 max), in addition
to shared authority data and any retained outputs. 𝑆max can be quadratic in source-family sizes. A small measured
cache-size counter would not establish an unconditional linear-memory bound or a process peak bound.
6.4 Adaptive selection
The frozen dispatcher considers QUEUE-DENSE, ARWLF, and K2-WIDE. It checks the queued reporter’s capacity,
admits ARWLF only when its width and packet-amortization rules hold, and probes the legal candidates on a common
selected subset. K2-WIDE probes a prefix of source blocks, while ARWLF probes a prefix of nonempty target leaves.
Predictions scale preparation and sweep work separately. The smallest finite predicted time determines the backend;
ties retain a fixed order.
A selected ARWLF run can be guarded against the runner-up’s predicted break-even time per logical pair. After
a sufficient prefix, an unfavorable estimate aborts it and the runner-up starts from cleared output. This is a practical
heuristic, not a universal regret theorem. Probe overhead matters most when the selected problem is tiny, and the
admission threshold can exclude profitable cases. Those effects are part of the results rather than hidden by reporting
only the backend ultimately selected.

7.2 Controlled geometry and target density

Target thinning retains only entries of𝑌𝑢 at fractions one-half, one-quarter, and one-eighth, with source families

transformed input for all backends. Neither transformation is included in the authentic headline.
7.3 Machine, protocol, and timed boundary
Measurements use an Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX, pinned to P-core logical CPU 23, on AC power with EcoQoS disabled.
The program is C++20, compiled in MSVC Release mode; the recorded compiler identifier is 195136257. Optimization
uses /O2 and /Ob2 with native AVX2 translation units. All backends execute single-threaded on identical prepared
inputs.
Every case begins with an untimed exactness pass. The final protocol uses three warmup rounds and seven timed
rounds; the discovery protocol uses one warmup and three timed rounds. Backend order rotates by round and reverses
on odd rounds. Anchors, dimension-72 whole roots, and crossover confirmations use the final protocol. Strata and
most thinning cases use discovery. The pooled authentic headline therefore combines these two declared protocols; the
final-protocol anchors are also reported separately.
Timing includes the backend call, its lazy internal preparation, survivor resolution, shared sink processing, and
dispatcher probes where applicable. Snapshot loading, common dataset conversion, full-key and common-index
preparation, exactness passes, census, and post-call witness sorting are outside the prepared-backend comparison. A
repeated pass rebuilds ARWLF’s per-call cache; it does not reuse an uncharged persistent leaf index. Prepared-backend
timings do not include complete TER list generation.
A CPU sentinel records machine conditions at process boundaries and during the campaign. Some resumed source-
thinning points have slower sentinel readings; their matched ratios are retained. No accepted sample is recorded off the
pinned core. This is one processor and one implementation toolchain, not a hardware-portability study.

7.4 Exactness gates and statistics
For each timed case, sorted residue-exact identity multisets agree with the K2 reference. Intermediate width-count
and hash gates compare ARWLF with K2-WIDE and K2’s narrower checks; a separately computed census agrees with
counted geometry. Structural counts, identity hashes, directory misses, and residual mismatches are checked at their
declared levels. Synthetic tests add whole-value modular brute-force comparisons and scalar/vector filter comparisons,
including carry seams and overflow leaves.
All 221 completed campaign cases pass the recorded gates. Three source-thinning targets are unreachable, leaving
224 declared cases. The accepted timing set contains 3,220 samples; nine rows from incomplete cases are excluded from
summaries and retained as partial evidence. All authentic and thinned campaign cases have zero full-quotient matches,
residue-exact triples, and structural outputs. Agreement at those output gates is therefore supplemented by intermediate
counts, independent bounded tests, and the constructed positive experiments below. Zero-output agreement alone
would not validate recovery.

7.5 Constructed outputs and full prepared roots
A separate experiment appends controlled target records to one authentic cell. For a selected authentic source pair,
a constructed target has the full key and residue needed for arithmetic equality, and coordinate data chosen so the
summed parent passes the attached finite test-parent predicate. A decoy condition appends the same number of target
records without valid final outputs. The original source families and authentic target records are retained.
These appended records have ternary coordinate data but are not established members of the native level-one TER
sets. Their stored residues and coordinate completions are assigned separately; equality with the coordinate dot product
of the original weights is not established. The completion targets the attached finite test-parent predicate, not the binary
production root. They test arithmetic filtering and identity recovery under prescribed output loads, and do not create
authentic solver solutions. The completed repeated subset available for this paper comprises one calibration root and
three samples per case. Other scheduled roots are not included as if their runs had finished.
The complete dimension-108 prepared-root experiment uses seed 0x1001, all 4,096 cells, and one constructed positive
target. ARWLF and the dispatcher run in one call to retain their within-call family caches. Each available completed pass
is a single measurement, without the campaign warmup and repeated-round protocol. The baseline run is checkpointed
by target family and is incomplete at the evidence cutoff. Its partial time is not extrapolated into a measured whole-root
speedup.

Controlled source thinning changes the amortization without changing the quotient width or target family. At𝐿= 1,

As source occupancy increases, ARWLF’s advantage grows. This is a finite-regime crossover observed under this

8.6 A complete dimension-108 prepared root
The completed ARWLF pass covers 25,189,929,333,072 logical pairs across all 4,096 cells and reports the prescribed
single identity triple. Its backend time is 3,575.458 seconds, or 59.591 minutes. The dispatcher pass selects ARWLF,
reports the identical residue-exact triple and structural count, and takes 3,557.074 seconds, or 59.285 minutes. Measured
process peaks are 3,278.5 and 3,278.6 MiB. Table 5 separates setup and backend time.
The slightly shorter dispatcher pass is not evidence that probing improves the kernel; these are separate single passes
under different machine conditions. ARWLF’s amortized time is approximately 0.142 ns per logical pair, including its
within-call preparation and authority work. This normalization represents many-lane processing, not subnanosecond
scalar execution.
The first ARWLF pass is unguarded, and the protocol records background activity and Windows page compression. No
repeated-run dispersion estimate is available for these completed passes. They establish that this prepared full-root scope
completes and handles the constructed output on the measured machine. A completed baseline whole-root comparison,
a representative multi-root whole-root campaign, and an end-to-end TER solve remain outside the measured result.

9 Relation to prior work
9.1 Subset Sum representations and exact algorithms
Horowitz and Sahni split an instance into two parts and compare enumerated subset sums, establishing the classical
meet-in-the-middle baseline [8]. Schroeppel and Shamir preserve the half-exponent time scale while reducing storage
by generating ordered pair sums from four smaller lists [ 13]. These are general exact approaches. ARWLF neither
replaces their worst-case algorithms nor implements a Schroeppel-Shamir recovery structure; it optimizes a three-list
merge arising inside a specific representation construction.
Howgrave-Graham and Joux introduce multiple representations to exploit hard random knapsacks [9]. Becker, Coron,
and Joux extend the technique using signed entries and filtering, and analyze a heuristic2 0.291𝑛 time regime [5]. Their
explicit treatment of pre-filter intermediate lists is particularly relevant here: the cost of rejected arithmetic candidates
cannot be inferred from the final valid list. Bonnetain, Bricout, Schrottenloher, and Shen analyze improved classical and
quantum representation algorithms [6]. Li’s later TER construction uses ternary merges and a depth-three topology,
with a heuristic random density-one time bound2 0.2400𝑛 [10]. The finite-width reporter in this paper operates within
such list geometry; it does not prove or improve those heuristic exponents.
Nederlof and Węgrzycki use representations and orthogonal vectors to improve Schroeppel and Shamir’s space
exponent in a Monte Carlo algorithm for worst-case instances, while retaining its half-exponent time scale [11]. Chen,
Jin, Randolph, and Servedio obtain a randomized polynomial-factor improvement over the meet-in-the-middle time
bound for worst-case Subset Sum using bit packing and representation methods [ 7]. Their word width grows with
the instance encoding; a fixed sixteen-lane instruction alone would not establish that asymptotic improvement. These
results demonstrate that word-level computation and representation ideas can yield theoretical improvements when
supported by an asymptotic analysis. ARWLF’s fixed sixteen-lane row sweep is instead a measured constant-factor
engineering result, with explicit survivor costs and no claimed improvement of the general exact bound.
9.2 Packed reporting and machine-oriented joins

and word-level parallelism [3]. Barba and coauthors study algebraic generalizations of 3SUM under real-RAM and
algebraic decision-tree models, distinguishing decision from output-sensitive reporting [4]. A modular three-list equality
resembles these settings arithmetically, but the measured TER workload also carries original-entry multiplicity and

10 Discussion and limits
The evidence supports a simple design lesson: when a short projection creates a flood of candidates, moving high-
information necessary conditions ahead of recovery can matter more than reducing the apparent count of valid outputs.
K2-WIDE shows that early full-width rejection alone helps; ARWLF shows that a much cheaper filter and a different
row organization can improve further. Target thinning then connects the gain to the baseline’s projected-candidate
sensitivity. Together these comparisons explain the architectural result without assigning the entire speedup to AVX2.
The dense advantage has a boundary. Sparse leaves waste lanes and expose preparation cost. Duplicated or highly
correlated target mids can saturate independent byte maps, enlarge the survivor buffer, and increase leaf scans.
Constructed output stress exercises that path, but remains a one-root controlled experiment. Memory and time should
therefore be predicted from entries, row occupancy, survivor counts, target-leaf sizes, and output multiplicity together.
A single list-size threshold is insufficient.
The holdout design is stronger than reporting a single favorable anchor, but modest in independent-instance count.
Its fifteen holdout cells derive from three roots on one processor. The root-block intervals respect that clustering,
yet cannot establish processor-independent performance or all-density behavior. Additional machines and complete
repeated-root comparisons would answer distinct questions, rather than merely add more samples of the same cells.
The evaluated backend is also separate from production solving. Preparation captures authentic native lists, and the
arithmetic authority checks their stored modular residues. The benchmark’s finite test-parent predicate differs from the
binary production root and is incompatible with authentic root digit sums. A full integration must use the production
predicate, preserve complete list-generation and failure semantics, recover and deduplicate parents as required, and
independently recompute any claimed binary witness against the original instance. None of those end-to-end costs
can be inferred from a prepared-cell ratio. The complete prepared-root pass establishes arithmetic-reporting feasibility
at that boundary, while the incomplete baseline comparison remains an open measurement rather than an estimated
result.
